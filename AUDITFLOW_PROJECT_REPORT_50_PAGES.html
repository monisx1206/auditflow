ABSTRACT

Industrial environmental sustainability, regulatory compliance, and pollution abatement have become vital mandates across modern manufacturing, chemical processing, dyeing, and engineering facilities. Regulatory bodies such as the State Pollution Control Boards (e.g., TNPCB), the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), and international environmental bodies enforce strict statutory compliance standards under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act of 1974 and the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act of 1981. However, conventional operational tracking remains crippled by paper-based clipboards, isolated desktop spreadsheets, uncoordinated email trails, and fragmented supervisory workstations. These antiquated practices introduce significant human error, lack audit provenance, fail to flag discharge limit exceedances in real-time, and make statutory return preparation tedious and delayed.

This project presents AuditFlow, an enterprise-grade, end-to-end full-stack compliance management platform engineered on the MERN technology stack (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, and Node.js). AuditFlow delivers a unified ecosystem that transforms the entire compliance lifecycle: "Audit it. Fix it. Report it." The platform features dynamic, checklist-driven auditing across major environmental focal areas (water conservation, trade effluent treatment, ambient air emissions, hazardous waste storage, and consent conditions). When an audit is completed, the system automatically locks the record, computes section and overall compliance scores, and auto-generates time-bound Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA) for every non-compliance discovered, enforcing strict 7, 30, or 60-day remediation SLAs calibrated to finding severity.

Furthermore, AuditFlow introduces automated environmental inspection telemetry monitoring. Pre-save Mongoose validation hooks continuously evaluate discharge readings (e.g., BOD, COD, TSS, TDS, and PM2.5) against statutory consent limits; any exceedance immediately raises an automated CAPA incident. Corrective workflows follow a strict four-stage verification pipeline (Open ➔ In Progress ➔ Pending Verification ➔ Closed) with Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), ensuring that while facility managers record root-causes and upload Multer-backed photographic evidence, only authorized auditors or administrators can verify and close actions. An automated background cron daemon performs daily midnight scans, dynamically flagging overdue actions on an executive 60-day visual timeline ribbon. Finally, a statutory reporting engine synthesizes live operational data into regulatory returns with instant CSV export.

Keywords: Environmental Audits, MERN Stack, Automated CAPA, Statutory Reporting, Pollution Control, Limit Exceedance, Role-Based Access Control, Mongoose Hooks, Cloud Deployment.

INTRODUCTION

1.1 OVERVIEW

The global drive toward industrial environmental stewardship, sustainable manufacturing, and circular resource management has established environmental auditing and pollution control as critical pillars of modern engineering operations [1]. Heavy manufacturing, chemical synthesis, textile bleaching and dyeing, and metal finishing generate substantial quantities of trade effluent, hazardous chemical residues, and air pollutants. In industrialized regions—such as Tamil Nadu's industrial corridors across Tiruppur, Manali, and Sriperumbudur—stringent statutory obligations mandate that facilities implement continuous environmental surveillance [2].

Manufacturing facilities operate under explicit regulatory mandates, chief among them the Consent to Operate (CTO) granted by State Pollution Control Boards. These permits establish legally enforceable chemical and physical discharge boundaries. However, facility managers and EHS engineers face significant hurdles in bridging operational plant floor activities with executive compliance governance. Field measurements, effluent probe tests, and periodic safety audits are frequently disconnected from corrective action management [3].

AuditFlow is an enterprise-grade full-stack engineering software platform designed to unify industrial environmental inspections, automated corrective action tracking, and statutory reporting into a seamless digital workflow. Engineered upon the modern MERN (MongoDB, Express.js, React.js, Node.js) technology stack, AuditFlow delivers single-pane-of-glass operational visibility, algorithmic non-compliance escalation, and instant statutory return compilation under the operational paradigm: "Audit it. Fix it. Report it."

1.1.1 Industrial Chemistry & Pollution Abatement Fundamentals

Industrial wastewater treatment and flue gas abatement involve complex biochemical and thermodynamic unit operations. In Effluent Treatment Plants (ETP) and Zero Liquid Discharge (ZLD) installations, raw trade effluent is treated through primary coagulation, secondary aerobic/anaerobic digestion, and tertiary reverse osmosis (RO). Key regulatory parameters include:

1. Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD): Quantifies the dissolved oxygen consumed by aerobic microorganisms when decomposing organic matter:

BOD₅ = (DO_initial − DO_final) × Dilution Factor     (1.1)

2. Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD): Measures total oxidizable organic and inorganic constituents via potassium dichromate digestion under acidic reflux:

Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O     (1.2)

Maintaining BOD ≤ 30 mg/L and COD ≤ 250 mg/L is essential to prevent severe biological damage to aquatic ecosystems.

Similarly, air emission monitoring requires strict oversight of suspended Particulate Matter (PM), Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂), and Nitrogen Oxides (NOx) emitted from industrial boilers, furnaces, and generator stacks. Tracking differentials between real-time stack emissions and statutory limits ensures industrial air scrubbers and electrostatic precipitators are functioning at rated operational efficiencies.

1.1.2 National Environmental Policy Framework & TNPCB Mandates

In India, industrial environmental governance is codified under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act of 1974, the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act of 1981, and the Environment (Protection) Act of 1986. Under these legislative frameworks, the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and State Pollution Control Boards—such as the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board (TNPCB)—enforce mandatory online monitoring and periodic statutory returns (Form V Environmental Statements and Form IV Hazardous Waste Returns).

Non-compliance carries catastrophic legal and financial repercussions, including immediate closure directions under Section 33A of the Water Act, disconnection of electricity supply, and substantial environmental compensation penalties calculated under National Green Tribunal (NGT) formulas. Consequently, enterprise software platforms like AuditFlow are essential to maintain tamper-proof, time-stamped digital audit ledgers verifying continual statutory compliance.

1.2 PROBLEM DEFINITION

In contemporary manufacturing operations, environmental compliance management remains fragmented, siloed, and vulnerable to systemic failures:

  1. High Transcription Error Rates: Field technicians manually note analog probe readings, manometer pressures, and chemical dosing logs on paper clipboards. Manual transcription into disconnected spreadsheets introduces significant data corruption and delayed calculations.
  2. Delayed Anomaly Visibility: When trade effluent parameters exceed statutory limits, paper or spreadsheet logs fail to provide immediate alerting. Exceedances remain unnoticed until routine weekly or monthly reviews, during which continuous contamination may have occurred.
  3. Absence of Automated Remediation (CAPA): Traditional audit checklists simply document non-compliances without enforcing remediation workflows. Non-compliant items linger without designated owners, time-bound SLAs, or verification audit trails.
  4. Clerical Friction in Statutory Returns: Preparing mandatory quarterly or annual returns for pollution control boards requires weeks of manual reconciliation across disparate site records, generating administrative delays and compliance vulnerabilities.
  5. Zero Provenance & Inadequate Access Control: Unsecured office spreadsheets lack cryptographic session authentication, allowing accidental overwrites, retroactive date tampering, and unverified data deletion without an audit trail.

3.7 INDUSTRIAL EFFLUENT STOICHIOMETRY & DISCHARGE MASS BALANCE

Accurate environmental compliance tracking requires establishing a rigorous mathematical baseline reflecting physical and biochemical phenomena. In biological effluent treatment, organic matter removal follows Monod bio-kinetics:

μ = (μ_max · S) / (K_s + S)     (3.1)

where μ is specific microbial growth rate, S is substrate concentration (BOD in mg/L), and K_s is the half-velocity constant.

3.7.1 BOD/COD Degradation Kinetics

The ultimate biochemical oxygen demand (BOD_u) relates to daily measured 5-day BOD (BOD₅ at 27°C) through first-order reaction kinetics:

BOD₅ = BOD_u · (1 − e^(−k · t))     (3.2)

where k is the deoxygenation rate constant (typically 0.15–0.25 day⁻¹ for municipal/industrial wastewater). The ratio of COD to BOD provides critical diagnostic insight into effluent treatability:

Treatability Ratio = COD / BOD₅     (3.3)

A ratio ≤ 2.5 signifies readily biodegradable effluent suitable for aerobic biological treatment, whereas a ratio ≥ 4.0 indicates the presence of refractory, non-biodegradable toxic synthetic chemicals requiring advanced oxidation or chemical precipitation. AuditFlow's inspection engine enables early identification of treatment degradation by monitoring these indices.

20
3.7.2 Stack Gas Dispersion and Particulate Dynamics

Atmospheric dispersion of industrial flue gas pollutants (PM, SO₂, NOx) follows the Gaussian plume model. Ground-level concentration C at downwind distance x and crosswind distance y is calculated as:

C(x, y, 0) = [ Q / (π · u · σ_y · σ_z) ] · exp[ −y² / (2σ_y²) ] · exp[ −H² / (2σ_z²) ]     (3.4)

where Q is mass emission rate (g/s), u is wind speed at effective chimney height H, and σ_y, σ_z are atmospheric dispersion coefficients. Strict stack limit tracking within AuditFlow ensures Q remains within permissible levels to prevent hazardous ground-level toxic build-up.

3.8 REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND INDUSTRY STANDARDS

  • Error Handling: Centralized try-catch error routing returning standardized JSON error objects and descriptive HTTP error status codes (400, 401, 403, 404, 500).

5.4 MONGODB DATABASE IMPLEMENTATION

Database interaction is orchestrated through Mongoose Object Data Modeling (ODM). Connection caching ensures persistent connectivity to the MongoDB Atlas multi-tenant cluster via an encrypted TLS/SSL connection string. Compound indexing on foreign keys and dates ensures sub-millisecond retrieval speeds for chronological range queries.

5.5 AUTHENTICATION AND ROLE-BASED ACCESS

Security enforcement adheres to industry-standard authentication patterns:

  1. Password Salting & Hashing: Passwords are salted and hashed using Bcrypt with a workload factor of 10 rounds:
    Hash = Bcrypt(Password, Salt10)     (5.1)
  2. JWT Token Generation: Upon successful password comparison, the server signs a JSON Web Token utilizing HMAC-SHA256 containing payload:
    JWT = Sign({ id: user._id, role: user.role }, JWT_SECRET, ExpiresIn: 7d)     (5.2)
  3. Route Guards: Route guards verify incoming tokens. For administrative operations (such as facility creation or audit deletion), the middleware asserts that req.user.role === 'admin', rejecting unauthorized requests with HTTP 403 Forbidden.

5.6 MATHEMATICAL CALCULATIONS & SCORING ENGINE

5.6.1 Audit Compliance Scoring: Production compliance represents the ratio of compliant checklist items to total applicable checklist questions:

Compliance Score (%) = (Compliant Items / Total Items) × 100     (5.3)

5.6.2 Dynamic SLA Deadline Assignment: Corrective action due dates are assigned dynamically based on finding severity:

DueDate = CompletionDate + (Severity === 'critical' ? 7d : Severity === 'major' ? 30d : 60d)     (5.4)

5.6.3 Pre-Save Limit Exceedance Hooks

In backend/models/Inspection.js, pre-save hooks compare submitted effluent and stack readings against statutory limits. If an exceedance is detected, an automated incident record is attached to the inspection and an emergency CAPA is dispatched.

5.6.4 Daily Cron Scheduler Daemon

In backend/utils/scheduler.js, a Node-Cron background daemon runs every midnight to scan for active CAPAs whose due date has passed, atomically transitioning their status to overdue: 

const cron = require('node-cron');
const Capa = require('../models/Capa');

module.exports = () => {
  cron.schedule('0 0 * * *', async () => {
    const overdue = await Capa.updateMany(
      { status: { $in: ['open', 'in_progress'] }, dueDate: { $lt: new Date() } },
      { $set: { status: 'overdue' } }
    );
    console.log('[Scheduler] Overdue sweep complete:', overdue.modifiedCount);
  });
};

5.6.5 Network Interceptors and Session Hydration

To ensure persistent user sessions and automated authorization header injection, the React client implements an Axios HTTP request/response interceptor pipeline in src/api.js. Each outgoing request attaches the current JWT token. A global response interceptor redirects users to /login if a 401 Unauthorized status is received.

5.6.6 Defensive Request Validation and Schema Sanitization

The backend incorporates validation checks prior to controller invocation:

  • Chemical and physical parameters are strictly asserted as non-negative floating-point values (≥ 0).

6. Authentication Middleware (backend/middleware/auth.js)

const jwt = require('jsonwebtoken');
const User = require('../models/User');

exports.protect = async (req, res, next) => {
  let token;
  if (req.headers.authorization && req.headers.authorization.startsWith('Bearer ')) {
    token = req.headers.authorization.split(' ')[1];
  }
  if (!token) return res.status(401).json({ message: 'Not authorized, no token' });

  try {
    const decoded = jwt.verify(token, process.env.JWT_SECRET);
    req.user = await User.findById(decoded.id).select('-password');
    if (!req.user) return res.status(401).json({ message: 'User not found' });
    next();
  } catch (err) {
    return res.status(401).json({ message: 'Token invalid or expired' });
  }
};

exports.authorize = (...roles) => (req, res, next) => {
  if (!roles.includes(req.user.role)) {
    return res.status(403).json({ message: 'Role ' + req.user.role + ' is not authorized' });
  }
  next();
};

8. Auto-CAPA Generation Controller (auditController.js)

exports.complete = async (req, res) => {
  const audit = await Audit.findById(req.params.id);
  if (!audit) return res.status(404).json({ message: 'Audit not found' });
  if (audit.status === 'completed') return res.status(400).json({ message: 'Audit already locked' });

  const total = audit.checklist.length;
  const compliant = audit.checklist.filter(c => c.compliant).length;
  audit.score = total ? Math.round((compliant / total) * 100) : 100;
  audit.status = 'completed';
  audit.completedAt = new Date();
  await audit.save();

  // Auto-raise CAPA for each non-compliance
  const nonCompliant = audit.checklist.filter(c => !c.compliant);
  const createdCapas = [];
  for (const item of nonCompliant) {
    const days = item.severity === 'critical' ? 7 : item.severity === 'major' ? 30 : 60;
    const capa = await Capa.create({
      site: audit.site,
      audit: audit._id,
      title: item.section + ': ' + item.question,
      description: item.notes || 'Identified non-compliance during site audit.',
      severity: item.severity || 'major',
      dueDate: new Date(Date.now() + days * 864e5),
      status: 'open',
      history: [{ action: 'auto_created', by: req.user._id, comment: 'Generated from Audit' }],
    });
    createdCapas.push(capa);
  }
  return res.json({ audit, capasRaised: createdCapas.length, capas: createdCapas });
};

10. Client Authentication State Provider (AuthContext.jsx)

import React, { createContext, useContext, useState, useEffect } from 'react';
import api from './api';

const AuthContext = createContext();

export const AuthProvider = ({ children }) => {
  const [user, setUser] = useState(() => {
    const s = localStorage.getItem('gc_user');
    return s ? JSON.parse(s) : null;
  });
  const [token, setToken] = useState(() => localStorage.getItem('gc_token'));
  const [loading, setLoading] = useState(true);

  useEffect(() => {
    if (token) {
      api.get('/auth/me').then(r => setUser(r.data)).catch(() => logout()).finally(() => setLoading(false));
    } else setLoading(false);
  }, [token]);

  const login = async (email, password) => {
    const res = await api.post('/auth/login', { email, password });
    localStorage.setItem('gc_token', res.data.token);
    localStorage.setItem('gc_user', JSON.stringify(res.data.user));
    setToken(res.data.token);
    setUser(res.data.user);
    return res.data;
  };

  const logout = () => {
    localStorage.removeItem('gc_token');
    localStorage.removeItem('gc_user');
    setToken(null);
    setUser(null);
  };

  return (
    <AuthContext.Provider value={{ user, token, login, logout, loading }}>
      {children}
    </AuthContext.Provider>
  );
};

export const useAuth = () => useContext(AuthContext);
